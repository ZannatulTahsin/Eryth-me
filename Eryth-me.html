Reading the erythema z-score

  
The z-score is how many baseline “spreads” (standard deviations of EI across the baseline pixels) the target’s average EI sits above the baseline’s average EI. For example, +2 means the target is 2 spreads redder than the baseline. It describes statistical distance only and is not a clinical finding. The row matching your result is highlighted.

  

    
z-scoreWhat it means statistically

    
Below 0Target is less red than the baseline.

    
0 to 1Within the normal pixel-to-pixel variation of the baseline skin.

Above 3Far outside the baseline’s variation.

  

  
Lighting, shadows, skin texture and where you draw the boxes can also move the z-score, and a very uneven baseline (large spread) pulls it toward zero. Compare photos taken under the same conditions.

      Method: pixels are converted from sRGB to linear light, optionally balanced to the gray card, then MI = 100·log10(1/R) and EI = 100·log10(R/G) are computed per pixel. The z-score is the target mean EI minus the baseline mean EI, divided by the baseline pixel-to-pixel SD (floored at 0.5 to avoid division by near zero). Percent difference is (baseline EI − target EI) ÷ target EI, shown for reference only, and is unstable when target EI is near zero. A phone camera is not a calibrated spectrometer, so compare photos taken under the same conditions.
